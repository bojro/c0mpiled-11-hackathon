# Debrief — spoken pitch (Corey lead)

Hi! I'm Corey, partnered with Bojro. Tonight we're presenting **Debrief**.

As a student — and everyone building in this room will relate — applying for
jobs has become a cutthroat process. And that process begins with a résumé.

What started as an innocent way to narrate your experience has turned into a
mess of keyword spam, résumé lingo, and extravagant exaggeration that HR has
to wade through. Résumés pass the filter not because of the candidate's
skills, but because of the terminology they stuffed in.

This is where Debrief comes in. Debrief sits in the stack between the
applicant submitting a résumé and HR ever reading it. The moment you apply,
Debrief's voice agent conducts a real behavioral interview about *your*
résumé — the way a Bar Raiser would. It asks the follow-up questions a bullet
point can't dodge, cross-examines your claims against your GitHub, checks
your employment history with CrustData — live, while you're still talking —
and watches your story for drift.

And Debrief is built on observability. Here's what the recruiter sees.

**Green** survived interrogation — the answers showed real competence, and
the quotes and audio are right here as evidence. **Yellow** is the honest
middle — real work, but narrower than the phrasing implied; expand it and see
exactly why. **Red** collapsed under follow-up — the story drifted, and you
can *hear* it drift. **[play the red clip here]**

The résumé as we know it is dying — drowning in exaggeration that a recruiter
making a thirty-second decision has no way to check.
**[show the plain grey résumé]**

But now, that same résumé looks like this. **[show the marked-up résumé]**
The recruiter sees instantly what held up and what didn't — and every mark
opens into the evidence behind it.

The résumé stopped being signal. The conversation is the new résumé.
We're Debrief. Thank you.
